The entire history of rome - The Cesers

753 bce: According to tradition, Romulus and Remus found Rome; the city is ruled by a series of kings.
509 bce: Roman monarchy is replaced by elected officials.
133–31 bce: This period of internal strife and civil wars encompasses the careers of Sulla, Pompey, and Julius Caesar. Other events during this time include Spartacus’s rebellion.
44 bce: Julius Caesar is assassinated.
43–32 bce: Rome is ruled by the Second Triumvirate of Mark Antony, Lepidus, and Octavian.
31 bce: Octavian defeats Mark Antony and Cleopatra in the Battle of Actium, gaining complete control of Roman territory. Egypt officially becomes a Roman province in 30 bce, joining other parts of North Africa, Italy, Gaul, much of present-day Spain, Greece, Asia Minor, and Syria.
The principate (27 bce–284 ce)
Julio-Claudian dynasty
28 bce: Octavian is given the title of princeps, or “first citizen,” by the Senate.

27 bce: Octavian is elevated as Augustus Caesar and granted immense power by the Senate. Over the following years, he brings Spain fully under Roman rule and reorganizes the administration of Spain and Gaul.

23 bce: The Senate gives Augustus complete control, officially ending the Republic. He establishes the principate, which is derived from princeps and is a form of government blending traditional monarchy with elements from the Republic. Rome enjoys two centuries of peace under the Pax Romana, which lasts through the reign of Marcus Aurelius (161–180 ce).

12 bce: Roman commander Nero Claudius Drusus Germanicus begins a series of campaigns in German territory and occupies land between the Rhine and Elbe rivers.

6 ce: Judaea becomes a Roman province after Augustus deposes Herod Archelaus, son of Herod the Great.

9 ce: German leader Arminius defeats three Roman legions under Publius Quinctilius Varus in the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest, checking the Roman advance into German territory beyond the Rhine.

14–37 ce: Tiberius is made emperor after the death of Augustus, his adoptive father. Rome prospers under Tiberius, who is an efficient ruler. He annexes Cappadocia in 17 ce but becomes a tyrant in his final years.

Caligula
CaligulaMarble bust of Roman emperor Caligula; in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York City.
37 ce: Caligula, son of Tiberius’s nephew and adoptive son, Germanicus Caesar, accedes to the throne after Tiberius dies. He proves to be tyrannical and extravagant, claiming divinity and showing signs of insanity. He is popularly believed to have made his horse a consul, an account that is likely untrue.

41 ce: Claudius becomes emperor after Caligula, his nephew, is assassinated by members of his Praetorian Guard.

43 ce: Claudius begins the conquest of Britain by invading the southeastern part of the island. Londinium (London) is founded, and roads are built in 47–50 ce.

About 44 ce: Claudius annexes Mauretania, which had been ruled by Roman vassals since the late 2nd century bc.

54 ce: Nero accedes to the throne after his mother, Julia Agrippina (Agrippina the Younger), poisons Claudius, who was both her uncle and her husband. She is, in turn, murdered on Nero’s orders in 59 ce. This is the end of the line of ceser. the story continues on the next page
