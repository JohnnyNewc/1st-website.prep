Byzantine, or Eastern Roman, Empire

The Byzantine Empire at the death of Justinian I in 565 ce
The Eastern Empire continues for nearly 1,000 years. Key events include the following:


482 ce: Emperor Zeno issues the Henotikon (Greek: “Edict of Union”), an edict that aims to reconcile two opposing factions of Christianity. However, this causes the Acacian Schism with Pope Felix III, which lasts from 484–519 ce.
527–565 ce: During his reign Justinian I attempts to regain the Western Empire, sponsors a set of laws known as the Code of Justinian, and builds the Hagia Sophia.
610–641 ce: During the reign of Heraclius the official language is changed from Latin to Greek, and the empire achieves a decisive victory in the Byzantine-Sasanian war (602–628 ce), the final phase in the Roman struggle against the Persians. Arab conquests begin about 633 ce, and the empire loses Egypt, Syria, and Palestine.
726 ce: Iconoclasm, or the banning of religious images, begins under Leo III and becomes official policy in 730 ce. It ends in 843 ce under Empress Theodora (widow of Theophilus) and is celebrated as the Feast of Orthodoxy.
867–1025 ce: The declining empire revives during the Macedonian renaissance, a period of cultural and military progress. Expansions are made in Eastern Europe.
1054 ce: The East-West Schism between the Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches, respectively led by the patriarch of Constantinople and the pope, marks a watershed moment in church history.
1095–1261 ce: The empire plays a complex role during the Crusades. The First Crusade takes place as a result of Emperor Alexius I Comnenus’s appeal for Western help against Seljuq expansion into Byzantine territory. There is an erosion of trust between the empire and the West over the next Crusades, culminating in the sack of Constantinople during the Fourth Crusade in 1204 ce. The Latin empire is established from territory seized from the Byzantines. Constantinople is regained, and Byzantine rule, now greatly weakened, is restored in 1261 ce.
1354 ce: Turkish tribes cross into Europe and make incursions into the disintegrating empire, which is fragmented by civil war.
1453 ce: Mehmed II takes Constantinople, ending Byzantine rule and expanding the Ottoman Empire.
